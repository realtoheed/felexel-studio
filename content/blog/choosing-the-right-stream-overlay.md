Your overlay is the first thing a new viewer notices, and the thing your regulars
stare at for hours. Getting it right is less about packing in features and more
about knowing what to leave out.

## Start with your content, not the art

A **Just Chatting** stream needs room for a large camera and readable chat. A
competitive FPS needs the edges of the screen kept clear so nothing covers the
minimap or ammo counter. Decide what has to stay visible before anyone starts
drawing.

## Keep the palette tight

Two or three colours is plenty. Your alerts and emotes should be able to pop
against the overlay — if the frame is already loud, nothing else can stand out.

## Design for the small screen

More than half of Twitch traffic is mobile. Zoom your mock-up down to phone size:
if the labels are unreadable, they are decorative, and decorative elements should
be smaller and quieter.

## Build it as a set

An overlay on its own is not a brand. Panels, alerts, starting-soon and BRB
screens, and a matching emote set are what make a channel feel deliberate. Plan
them together, even if you order them in stages.
