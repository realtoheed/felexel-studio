A mascot gives your audience something to recognise instantly — in a sub badge,
on a thumbnail, on a sticker on someone's laptop. That is hard to achieve with
type alone.

## It survives being shrunk

A good mascot reads at 28×28 pixels as a Twitch badge and at poster size on
merch. Test yours at the smallest size it will ever appear before you approve it.

## It gives you a whole cast

Once a character exists, the emote set writes itself: happy, angry, crying,
hyped. You are no longer designing eight unrelated icons — you're drawing one
character eight times, and the set feels cohesive automatically.

## It carries tone without words

A rounded, soft mascot signals a cosy stream. Sharp angles and a scowl signal
competitive. Viewers pick this up in a fraction of a second, long before they
read your channel description.

## It outlives a redesign

Layouts, overlays and colour schemes change every year or two. A well-drawn
character can be re-rendered in a new style and still be the same recognisable
face — the equity you build in it compounds.
